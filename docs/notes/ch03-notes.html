Chapter 3 — Parallel & Perpendicular Lines

A transversal cutting parallel lines creates eight angles with predictable relationships. Mastering these relationships — both directions: "if parallel, then…" and its converse "if these angles match, then parallel" — is the core skill of Chapter 3.

1 Vocabulary

Parallel lines (∥)
Coplanar lines that never intersect. Same slope.
Skew lines
Lines that are not coplanar; they neither intersect nor are parallel.
Parallel planes
Planes that never intersect.
Transversal
A line that crosses two (or more) coplanar lines at distinct points.
Perpendicular (⊥)
Lines intersecting at a right angle.
Distance from a point to a line
The length of the perpendicular segment from the point to the line.

2 Transversal Angle Pairs

When a transversal crosses two lines it forms eight angles. They are grouped into four named pairs.

PairPositionIf lines are ∥, then…
CorrespondingSame side of transversal, same vertical position (one above each line, one below each line).Congruent
Alternate InteriorBetween the two lines, on opposite sides of the transversal.Congruent
Alternate ExteriorOutside the two lines, on opposite sides of the transversal.Congruent
Consecutive Interior (Co-interior, Same-Side Interior)Between the two lines, on the same side of the transversal.Supplementary (sum 180°)

3 Theorems — "If Parallel, Then…"

Postulate 3-1
Corresponding Angles Postulate
If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, then each pair of corresponding angles is congruent.
Theorem 3-1
Alternate Interior Angles Theorem
If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, then each pair of alternate interior angles is congruent.
Theorem 3-2
Consecutive Interior Angles Theorem
If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, then each pair of consecutive interior angles is supplementary.
Theorem 3-3
Alternate Exterior Angles Theorem
If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, then each pair of alternate exterior angles is congruent.
Theorem 3-4
Perpendicular Transversal Theorem
If a transversal is perpendicular to one of two parallel lines, then it is perpendicular to the other.

4 Converses — "Then Parallel"

These converses let you prove two lines are parallel.

Postulate 3-2
Converse — Corresponding Angles
If two coplanar lines are cut by a transversal so a pair of corresponding angles is congruent, then the lines are parallel.
Theorem 3-5
Converse — Alternate Interior
If a pair of alternate interior angles is congruent, then the lines are parallel.
Theorem 3-6
Converse — Consecutive Interior
If a pair of consecutive interior angles is supplementary, then the lines are parallel.
Theorem 3-7
Converse — Alternate Exterior
If a pair of alternate exterior angles is congruent, then the lines are parallel.
Theorem 3-8
Transitive Property of Parallel Lines
If a ∥ b and b ∥ c, then a ∥ c.
Theorem 3-9
Two Lines ⊥ Same Line ⇒ Parallel
In a plane, two lines perpendicular to the same line are parallel to each other.

5 Perpendicular Lines

Theorem 3-10
Perpendicular Lines Form Congruent Adjacent Angles
If two lines are perpendicular, then they form four congruent right angles.
Postulate 3-3
Parallel Postulate (Euclid's 5th)
Through a point not on a line, there is exactly one line parallel to the given line.
Postulate 3-4
Perpendicular Postulate
Through a point not on a line, there is exactly one line perpendicular to the given line.
Distance from a point to a line is measured along the perpendicular segment from the point to the line. Parallel lines remain a constant perpendicular distance apart.

6 Slopes — Parallel & Perpendicular

m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁) = rise / run
RelationshipSlope condition
Parallel (non-vertical)m₁ = m₂ — same slope
Perpendicular (neither vertical)m₁ · m₂ = −1 (slopes are negative reciprocals)
Vertical linex = c · slope undefined
Horizontal liney = c · slope = 0
Horizontal & verticalAlways perpendicular to each other.
Quick check for perpendicular: flip and negate. The perpendicular to slope 2/3 has slope −3/2.

Example · Writing a Parallel Line
Find the equation of the line through (2, −1) parallel to y = 3x + 4.
  1. Parallel ⇒ same slope: m = 3.
  2. Point-slope: y − (−1) = 3(x − 2) → y + 1 = 3x − 6.
  3. Solve: y = 3x − 7.
y = 3x − 7
Example · Writing a Perpendicular Line
Find the line through (4, 2) perpendicular to y = ½x − 3.
  1. Original slope ½ ⇒ perpendicular slope −2.
  2. Point-slope: y − 2 = −2(x − 4).
  3. Simplify: y = −2x + 10.
y = −2x + 10

8 Worked Examples

Example 1 · Find missing angles
Lines ℓ and m are parallel cut by transversal t. One angle measures 110°. Find the other seven.
  1. Vertical to 110° ⇒ 110°.
  2. Linear pair with 110° ⇒ 70°.
  3. Corresponding angles on the second line repeat the same pattern (110°, 70°, 110°, 70°).
Four angles 110°, four angles 70°.
Example 2 · Solve for x given parallel lines
Co-interior angles measure (2x + 30)° and (3x + 20)°. Find x.
  1. Co-interior ⇒ supplementary: (2x+30) + (3x+20) = 180.
  2. 5x + 50 = 180 → 5x = 130 → x = 26.
x = 26
Example 3 · Prove lines parallel
Show that the line through (1, 2) and (4, 8) is parallel to the line through (0, −1) and (2, 3).
  1. Slope 1: (8 − 2)/(4 − 1) = 6/3 = 2.
  2. Slope 2: (3 − (−1))/(2 − 0) = 4/2 = 2.
  3. Slopes equal ⇒ lines parallel.
m₁ = m₂ = 2 ⇒ parallel ✓

9 Common Pitfalls

  • The angle-pair relationships only hold when the lines are parallel. Don't assume corresponding angles are congruent if you haven't been told (or shown) the lines are parallel.
  • Skew ≠ parallel. Skew lines are non-coplanar and have no slope relationship in 2-D.
  • Co-interior (consecutive interior) angles are supplementary, not congruent.
  • For perpendicular slopes, the product is −1 (a sign flip and a reciprocal). Just flipping or just negating isn't enough.
  • Vertical lines have undefined slope — they are perpendicular to horizontal lines (slope 0). The product rule doesn't apply.
  • When labeling, write "a ∥ b" not "a = b".

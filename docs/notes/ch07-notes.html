Chapter 7 — Right Triangles & Trigonometry

Right triangles unlock a powerful toolkit: the Pythagorean Theorem, the two special right triangles, and the trigonometric ratios sine, cosine, and tangent. Together they let you find any missing side or angle from limited information.

1 Vocabulary

Hypotenuse
The side opposite the right angle — always the longest side of a right triangle.
Legs
The two sides forming the right angle.
Adjacent side
The leg next to the reference acute angle (not the hypotenuse).
Opposite side
The leg across from the reference acute angle.
Angle of elevation
The angle from the horizontal up to a line of sight.
Angle of depression
The angle from the horizontal down to a line of sight.
Geometric mean
The positive value x with a/x = x/b, i.e. x = √(ab).

2 Geometric Mean & Right-Triangle Altitudes

Theorem 7-1
Right-Triangle Altitude → 3 Similar Triangles
The altitude drawn from the right-angle vertex to the hypotenuse divides the right triangle into two triangles, each similar to the original and to each other.

Let the altitude h divide the hypotenuse into segments p (adjacent to leg a) and q (adjacent to leg b), so p + q = c.

Theorem 7-2
Geometric Mean (Altitude)
The altitude to the hypotenuse is the geometric mean of the two segments it creates: h = √(p · q).
Theorem 7-3
Geometric Mean (Leg)
Each leg is the geometric mean of the hypotenuse and the segment of the hypotenuse adjacent to that leg: a = √(c · p), b = √(c · q).

3 Pythagorean Theorem & Its Converse

Theorem 7-4
Pythagorean Theorem
In a right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the legs.
a² + b² = c² (where c is the hypotenuse)
Theorem 7-5
Converse of the Pythagorean Theorem
If a² + b² = c² for the three sides of a triangle (with c the longest), then the triangle is a right triangle.

4 Pythagorean Triples & Triangle Classification

Whole-number side lengths that satisfy a² + b² = c². Memorize the common ones — they appear constantly on tests.

  • 3 – 4 – 5  (and 6-8-10, 9-12-15, …)
  • 5 – 12 – 13
  • 8 – 15 – 17
  • 7 – 24 – 25
  • 20 – 21 – 29
Theorem 7-6
Triangle Classification by Sides
Let c be the longest side of a triangle with sides a, b, c:
  • c² = a² + b² → Right triangle.
  • c² > a² + b² → Obtuse triangle.
  • c² < a² + b² → Acute triangle.

5 Special Right Triangles

Theorem 7-7
45°–45°–90° Triangle
In a 45°-45°-90° (isosceles right) triangle, the hypotenuse is √2 times the length of each leg.
leg : leg : hyp = 1 : 1 : √2
Theorem 7-8
30°–60°–90° Triangle
In a 30°-60°-90° triangle: hypotenuse is 2× shorter leg; longer leg is √3 × shorter leg.
short : long : hyp = 1 : √3 : 2
Map the side to the angle opposite it: 30° → 1 (shortest), 60° → √3, 90° → 2 (hypotenuse).

6 Trigonometric Ratios (SOH-CAH-TOA)

For an acute angle θ in a right triangle:

sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse
cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent

Reciprocal ratios (less common at this level):

csc θ = 1/sin θ , sec θ = 1/cos θ , cot θ = 1/tan θ

Inverse trig — finding the angle

To find an angle when you know the ratio, use the inverse function on your calculator:

θ = sin⁻¹(opp/hyp) , θ = cos⁻¹(adj/hyp) , θ = tan⁻¹(opp/adj)
Make sure your calculator is in DEGREE mode (not radian) for geometry problems.

7 Solving a Right Triangle

Solving a triangle means finding all unknown sides and angles. In a right triangle, you need either:

  • Two side lengths → use Pythagorean & inverse trig, or
  • One side & one acute angle → use sin / cos / tan.

8 Angles of Elevation & Depression

The angle of elevation from point A to point B equals the angle of depression from B down to A — they are alternate interior angles formed by the line of sight and the two horizontals.

When setting up a problem, draw a horizontal at the eye/ground line and label the line of sight. The angle is always measured from the horizontal.

9 Law of Sines & Law of Cosines

For triangles that aren't right triangles:

Theorem 7-9
Law of Sines
In any triangle, sin A / a = sin B / b = sin C / c.
Use when you know AAS, ASA, or SSA (two angles + a side, or two sides + a non-included angle).
Theorem 7-10
Law of Cosines
In any triangle, c² = a² + b² − 2ab·cos C (and the cyclic versions for a, b).
Use when you know SAS (two sides + included angle) or SSS (all three sides, to find an angle).
If C = 90°, then cos C = 0, and the Law of Cosines reduces to the Pythagorean Theorem.

10 Worked Examples

Example 1 · Pythagorean Theorem
Right triangle legs 9 and 12. Find the hypotenuse.
  1. c² = 81 + 144 = 225.
  2. c = 15 (a 3-4-5 triple scaled by 3).
c = 15
Example 2 · 45-45-90
An isosceles right triangle has a leg of 8. Find the hypotenuse.
  1. Ratio 1 : 1 : √2 ⇒ hypotenuse = 8√2 ≈ 11.31.
8√2
Example 3 · 30-60-90
In a 30-60-90 triangle, the shorter leg is 5. Find the other two sides.
  1. Hypotenuse = 2·5 = 10.
  2. Longer leg = 5√3 ≈ 8.66.
10 and 5√3
Example 4 · Trig ratio
Find x: opposite leg 7, angle 35°, find the hypotenuse.
  1. Want hypotenuse given opposite & angle ⇒ use sin: sin 35° = 7/h.
  2. h = 7/sin 35° ≈ 7/0.5736 ≈ 12.20.
h ≈ 12.20
Example 5 · Inverse trig
Right triangle with legs 5 and 12. Find the angle opposite the leg of length 5.
  1. Opposite = 5, Adjacent = 12 ⇒ tan θ = 5/12.
  2. θ = tan⁻¹(5/12) ≈ 22.6°.
θ ≈ 22.6°
Example 6 · Law of Cosines
In △ABC, a = 7, b = 9, C = 60°. Find c.
  1. c² = 7² + 9² − 2(7)(9)cos 60° = 49 + 81 − 126(½) = 130 − 63 = 67.
  2. c = √67 ≈ 8.19.
c ≈ 8.19

11 Common Pitfalls

  • The Pythagorean Theorem only applies to right triangles. For other triangles use the Law of Cosines.
  • The hypotenuse is the side across from the right angle, always the longest.
  • Identify opposite and adjacent relative to the reference angle, not in a fixed way.
  • "Adjacent" never refers to the hypotenuse, even though the hypotenuse touches the angle.
  • Mixing up sin and cos: sin uses opposite, cos uses adjacent. Memorize SOH-CAH-TOA.
  • Calculator mode must be DEG for geometry. Radian mode gives wildly wrong answers.
  • In the 30-60-90 ratio, the "1" goes with the 30° angle (shortest), not the right angle.
  • Angle of elevation up = angle of depression down (alternate interior angles); they have the same measure.

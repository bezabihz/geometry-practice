Chapter 4 — Congruent Triangles

This chapter introduces the five shortcuts that prove two triangles congruent (SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, HL) and the strategy of using congruence to prove parts equal (CPCTC). Every later chapter — quadrilaterals, similarity, circles — rests on these tools.

1 Vocabulary & Classifications

By angles

Acute
All three angles less than 90°.
Equiangular
All three angles 60° (special case of acute).
Right
Exactly one 90° angle.
Obtuse
Exactly one angle greater than 90°.

By sides

Scalene
No congruent sides.
Isosceles
At least two congruent sides (the legs); the third side is the base.
Equilateral
All three sides congruent (and therefore equilateral ⇔ equiangular).

Key parts of a triangle

Vertex
The corner point.
Included side
The side between two angles.
Included angle
The angle between two sides.
Opposite side / angle
The side and angle that do not share a vertex/endpoint.

2 Triangle Angle Theorems

Theorem 4-1
Triangle Angle-Sum Theorem
The sum of the measures of the interior angles of a triangle is 180°.
Theorem 4-2
Exterior Angle Theorem
The measure of an exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two remote (non-adjacent) interior angles.
Why: Exterior + adjacent interior = 180°. The three interior angles also sum to 180°. Subtracting gives ext = sum of the other two interiors.
Corollary
Third-Angle Theorem (4-3)
If two angles of one triangle are congruent to two angles of another, the third angles are congruent. (Because both pairs sum to 180°.)
Corollary
Acute Angles of a Right Triangle
The two acute angles of a right triangle are complementary (sum to 90°).
Corollary
Equiangular Triangle
Each angle of an equiangular triangle measures 60°.

3 Congruent Polygons

Definition
Congruent polygons
Two polygons are congruent if their corresponding sides are congruent and their corresponding angles are congruent.
When you write △ABC ≅ △DEF, the order of letters matters: A↔D, B↔E, C↔F. Same goes for the corresponding sides: AB ≅ DE, BC ≅ EF, CA ≅ FD.

4 Triangle Congruence Postulates & Theorems

Instead of proving all six pairs (3 sides + 3 angles), you only need three of the right pieces.

Postulate 4-1
SSS — Side-Side-Side
If three sides of one triangle are congruent to three sides of another, the triangles are congruent.
Postulate 4-2
SAS — Side-Angle-Side
If two sides and the included angle of one triangle are congruent to the corresponding parts of another, the triangles are congruent.
Postulate 4-3
ASA — Angle-Side-Angle
If two angles and the included side of one triangle are congruent to the corresponding parts of another, the triangles are congruent.
Theorem 4-4
AAS — Angle-Angle-Side
If two angles and a non-included side of one triangle are congruent to the corresponding parts of another, the triangles are congruent.
Why it works: Two pairs of congruent angles force the third pair congruent (Third Angle Thm); then ASA applies.
SSA and AAA do NOT prove congruence.
  • SSA ("the ambiguous case") can produce two different triangles.
  • AAA proves only similar triangles (same shape, possibly different size).

5 CPCTC — Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent

Theorem
CPCTC
Once two triangles are proven congruent, every pair of corresponding parts — sides and angles — is congruent.
Strategy: To prove two segments or two angles congruent, find a pair of congruent triangles containing them. Prove the triangles ≅ first, then cite CPCTC for the specific parts.

6 Isosceles & Equilateral Triangles

Theorem 4-5
Isosceles Triangle Theorem (Base Angles Theorem)
If two sides of a triangle are congruent, then the angles opposite those sides are congruent.
Theorem 4-6
Converse of Isosceles Triangle Theorem
If two angles of a triangle are congruent, then the sides opposite those angles are congruent.
Theorem 4-7
Bisector of Vertex Angle
The bisector of the vertex angle of an isosceles triangle is the perpendicular bisector of the base.
Corollary
Equilateral ⇔ Equiangular
A triangle is equilateral if and only if it is equiangular (all 60°).

7 Right Triangle Congruence — HL

Theorem 4-8
HL — Hypotenuse-Leg Theorem
If the hypotenuse and a leg of one right triangle are congruent to the hypotenuse and a leg of another right triangle, the triangles are congruent.
HL is the only "SSA-like" rule that works — but it requires the right angle and the matched side be the hypotenuse. Outside of right triangles, SSA is invalid.

8 Worked Examples

Example 1 · Choose a congruence rule
In △ABC and △DEF, you know AB ≅ DE, ∠A ≅ ∠D, and AC ≅ DF. Which rule proves the triangles congruent?
  1. You have side, angle between, side.
  2. That's SAS (the angle is included between the two sides).
SAS
Example 2 · Exterior angle
In △ABC, ∠A = 40°, ∠B = 65°. Find the exterior angle at C.
  1. Exterior ∠ at C = sum of remote interiors = 40 + 65 = 105°.
105°
Example 3 · Isosceles triangle
In isosceles △ABC, AB ≅ AC. ∠B = 50°. Find ∠A.
  1. Base angles theorem: ∠B ≅ ∠C ⇒ ∠C = 50°.
  2. Angle sum: ∠A = 180 − 50 − 50 = 80°.
∠A = 80°

9 Common Pitfalls

  • SSA is not a congruence rule (only HL works in right triangles).
  • AAA proves similarity, not congruence.
  • The order of vertices in △ABC ≅ △DEF tells you the correspondence — don't randomize.
  • For SAS, the angle must be between the two sides; for ASA, the side must be between the two angles.
  • To use CPCTC, you must first prove the triangles congruent. CPCTC cannot be your first step.
  • Be wary of "vertical angles" and "shared sides" in diagrams — they are common implicit givens worth stating.
  • An "included" part touches both of the other two parts being cited.

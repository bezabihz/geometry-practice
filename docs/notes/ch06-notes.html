Chapter 6 — Proportions & Similarity

Similarity is congruence's flexible cousin: same shape, possibly different size. This chapter teaches how to set up proportions, recognize and prove similar triangles, and use the side-splitter and dilation tools.

1 Vocabulary

Ratio
A comparison of two quantities by division, written a : b or a/b (b ≠ 0).
Proportion
An equation stating two ratios are equal: a/b = c/d.
Extremes / Means
In a/b = c/d: a and d are extremes; b and c are means.
Similar figures (∼)
Same shape, corresponding angles ≅, corresponding sides proportional.
Scale factor (k)
The constant ratio between corresponding sides of similar figures.
Dilation
A transformation producing a similar figure; a scaling about a center point.
Geometric mean
For positive numbers a, b: x = √(ab) — the positive value satisfying a/x = x/b.

2 Properties of Proportions

If a/b = c/d (with all denominators non-zero), then each of the following is also true:

PropertyEquivalent form
Cross-productad = bc
Reciprocalb/a = d/c
Switchinga/c = b/d
Add-to-each-side(a+b)/b = (c+d)/d
Sum of ratiosIf a/b = c/d = e/f, then = (a+c+e)/(b+d+f).
When solving, cross-multiply only when both sides are single ratios. For a/b = c/d + e/f, simplify first.

3 Similar Polygons

Definition
Similar polygons
Two polygons are similar (∼) if their corresponding angles are congruent and their corresponding sides are proportional.
All corresponding sides share the same ratio — the scale factor k. If ABCD ∼ EFGH with scale factor 2/3, every side of ABCD is 2/3 the matching side of EFGH.

4 Proving Triangles Similar

Postulate 6-1
AA Similarity Postulate
If two angles of one triangle are congruent to two angles of another, the triangles are similar.
By the angle-sum theorem, two pairs of congruent angles force the third pair, so all three angles match.
Theorem 6-1
SSS Similarity
If the three pairs of corresponding sides of two triangles are proportional, the triangles are similar.
Theorem 6-2
SAS Similarity
If two pairs of corresponding sides are proportional and the included angles are congruent, the triangles are similar.
In similarity, the order of vertices encodes the correspondence just like with congruence: △ABC ∼ △DEF means A↔D, B↔E, C↔F.

5 Triangle Proportionality (Side-Splitter)

Theorem 6-3
Triangle Proportionality Theorem (Side-Splitter)
If a line parallel to one side of a triangle intersects the other two sides, it divides them proportionally.
If line ∥ BC meets AB at D and AC at E, then AD/DB = AE/EC.
Theorem 6-4
Converse — Side Splitter
If a line divides two sides of a triangle proportionally, then the line is parallel to the third side.
Corollary
Three Parallel Lines & Transversals
If three or more parallel lines are cut by two transversals, they divide the transversals proportionally.

6 Triangle Angle Bisector Theorem

Theorem 6-5
Triangle Angle Bisector Theorem
If a ray bisects an angle of a triangle, it divides the opposite side into segments proportional to the other two sides.
If AD bisects ∠A of △ABC and meets BC at D: BD/DC = AB/AC.

7 Dilations & Scale Factor

Definition
Dilation
A transformation that produces a similar image. Defined by a center C and a non-zero scale factor k. Each point P maps to P' on ray CP so that CP' = |k|·CP.
kEffect
|k| > 1Enlargement
0 < |k| < 1Reduction
|k| = 1Congruent (identity if k=1)
k < 0Enlargement / reduction through the center (rotated 180°)

Dilation centered at origin

(x, y) → (k·x, k·y)

Dilations preserve angle measures, parallel lines, and the shape of figures, but not distances (unless |k|=1).

8 Perimeter & Area Ratios in Similar Figures

Theorem 6-6
Perimeter & Area Ratios
If two similar polygons have scale factor a/b, then:
  • Ratio of perimeters = a/b.
  • Ratio of areas = a²/b².
Volume scaling (looking ahead to Ch 13): ratio of volumes = a³/b³. The exponent matches the dimension.

9 Worked Examples

Example 1 · Solve a proportion
Solve x/12 = 15/20.
  1. Cross-multiply: 20x = 12·15 = 180.
  2. x = 9.
x = 9
Example 2 · Similar triangles
△ABC ∼ △DEF with AB = 6, BC = 8, DE = 9. Find EF.
  1. Scale factor: DE/AB = 9/6 = 3/2.
  2. EF = (3/2)·BC = (3/2)·8 = 12.
EF = 12
Example 3 · Side-splitter
In △ABC, DE ∥ BC with D on AB and E on AC. AD = 4, DB = 6, AE = 5. Find EC.
  1. Side-splitter: AD/DB = AE/EC → 4/6 = 5/EC.
  2. EC = 5·6/4 = 7.5.
EC = 7.5
Example 4 · Area ratio
Two similar squares have side ratio 3:5. Find the ratio of their areas.
  1. Ratio of sides = 3/5.
  2. Ratio of areas = (3/5)² = 9/25.
9 : 25
Example 5 · Geometric mean
Find the geometric mean of 4 and 25.
  1. x = √(4·25) = √100 = 10.
x = 10

10 Common Pitfalls

  • Set up corresponding parts in the same order. Don't pair AB with EF if △ABC ∼ △DEF — pair AB with DE.
  • Area ratio is squared, not the same as the linear ratio.
  • The side-splitter ratio is AD/DB, not AD/AB. Watch the denominators.
  • For SAS similarity, the angle must be the included angle (between the two proportional sides).
  • Two similar figures are congruent only when the scale factor is 1.
  • Geometric mean is always positive; use only the positive square root.

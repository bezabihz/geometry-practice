Chapter 8 — Quadrilaterals

Quadrilaterals come in a family tree: a square is a rectangle and a rhombus, both of which are parallelograms. Knowing each shape's defining properties lets you classify figures, set up algebraic equations, and write proofs.

1 Vocabulary & Hierarchy

Polygon
A closed plane figure made of straight line segments.
Quadrilateral
A 4-sided polygon.
Parallelogram
A quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel.
Rectangle
A parallelogram with four right angles.
Rhombus
A parallelogram with four congruent sides.
Square
A parallelogram that is both a rectangle and a rhombus.
Trapezoid
A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides (the bases).
Isosceles trapezoid
A trapezoid with congruent legs.
Kite
A quadrilateral with two pairs of consecutive congruent sides and no parallel sides.
Diagonal
A segment connecting two non-adjacent vertices.
Hierarchy: Square ⊂ Rectangle ⊂ Parallelogram ⊂ Quadrilateral. Also Square ⊂ Rhombus ⊂ Parallelogram.

2 Polygon Angle Sums

Theorem 8-1
Polygon Interior Angle Sum
The sum of the interior angles of a convex polygon with n sides is (n − 2) · 180°.
Sum of interior ∠s = (n − 2) · 180°

Each interior angle of a regular polygon = (n − 2)·180° / n.

Theorem 8-2
Polygon Exterior Angle Sum
The sum of the measures of the exterior angles (one at each vertex) of any convex polygon is 360°.

Each exterior angle of a regular polygon = 360° / n.

3 Properties of a Parallelogram

If ABCD is a parallelogram, then:

Theorem 8-3
Opposite Sides ≅
Opposite sides of a parallelogram are congruent.
Theorem 8-4
Opposite Angles ≅
Opposite angles of a parallelogram are congruent.
Theorem 8-5
Consecutive Angles Supplementary
Consecutive (adjacent) angles of a parallelogram are supplementary.
Theorem 8-6
Diagonals Bisect Each Other
The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.

4 Proving a Quadrilateral is a Parallelogram

To prove a quadrilateral is a parallelogram, show any one of the following:

  • Theorem 8-7: Both pairs of opposite sides are congruent.
  • Theorem 8-8: Both pairs of opposite angles are congruent.
  • Theorem 8-9: One pair of opposite sides is both parallel and congruent.
  • Theorem 8-10: Diagonals bisect each other.
  • Definition: Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.

5 Rectangle

A rectangle has all the properties of a parallelogram plus:

Theorem 8-11
Rectangle — Four Right Angles
A parallelogram is a rectangle iff it has four right angles (equivalently, one right angle, since opposite ∠s are congruent and consecutive ∠s are supplementary).
Theorem 8-12
Rectangle Diagonals
The diagonals of a rectangle are congruent.
Theorem 8-13
Converse — Diagonals
If a parallelogram has congruent diagonals, then it is a rectangle.

6 Rhombus

A rhombus is a parallelogram with all sides ≅. Special diagonal properties:

Theorem 8-14
Rhombus Diagonals — Perpendicular
The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular.
Theorem 8-15
Rhombus Diagonals — Bisect Angles
Each diagonal of a rhombus bisects two opposite angles.
Theorem 8-16
Converse Tests
If a parallelogram has perpendicular diagonals OR has a diagonal that bisects opposite angles, then it is a rhombus.

7 Square

A square is both a rectangle and a rhombus. It has every property of both:

  • 4 right angles · 4 congruent sides.
  • Diagonals are congruent, perpendicular, bisect each other, and bisect the corner angles.
  • Each diagonal splits the square into two 45-45-90 right triangles.

8 Trapezoid & Midsegment

Definition
Trapezoid
A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides (the bases). The non-parallel sides are the legs.
Theorem 8-17
Isosceles Trapezoid — Base Angles
In an isosceles trapezoid, each pair of base angles is congruent.
Theorem 8-18
Isosceles Trapezoid — Diagonals
The diagonals of an isosceles trapezoid are congruent.
Theorem 8-19
Trapezoid Midsegment Theorem
The midsegment of a trapezoid (joining the midpoints of the legs) is parallel to the bases and its length is the average of the two bases.
midsegment = (b₁ + b₂) / 2

9 Kite

Theorem 8-20
Kite — Diagonals Perpendicular
The diagonals of a kite are perpendicular.
Theorem 8-21
Kite — One Diagonal Bisects the Other
Exactly one diagonal of a kite bisects the other. The diagonal connecting the vertices between equal pairs is the perpendicular bisector of the other diagonal.
Theorem 8-22
Kite — One Pair of Angles ≅
Exactly one pair of opposite angles in a kite is congruent (the two between the unequal sides).

10 Worked Examples

Example 1 · Interior sum
Find the sum of the interior angles of a 12-gon. Then find each interior angle if it is regular.
  1. Sum = (12 − 2)·180° = 1800°.
  2. Each interior = 1800° / 12 = 150°.
Sum 1800°; each 150°
Example 2 · Parallelogram algebra
In parallelogram ABCD, AB = 3x + 4, CD = 5x − 8. Find x and AB.
  1. Opposite sides ≅ ⇒ 3x + 4 = 5x − 8 → 12 = 2x → x = 6.
  2. AB = 3(6) + 4 = 22.
x = 6, AB = 22
Example 3 · Trapezoid midsegment
The bases of a trapezoid are 14 and 22. Find the midsegment.
  1. m = (14 + 22)/2 = 18.
m = 18
Example 4 · Classify by diagonals
A parallelogram has diagonals that are perpendicular but not congruent. Classify it.
  1. Perpendicular diagonals ⇒ rhombus.
  2. Not congruent ⇒ not a rectangle, so not a square.
Rhombus (not a square).

11 Common Pitfalls

  • A rhombus has perpendicular diagonals; a rectangle has congruent diagonals; a square has both.
  • Every square is a rectangle and a rhombus, but not every rectangle/rhombus is a square.
  • A trapezoid has exactly one pair of parallel sides (some textbooks allow "at least one" — Glencoe uses "exactly one").
  • A kite has two distinct pairs of consecutive ≅ sides — not opposite sides. (Two opposite-pairs would be a rhombus.)
  • "Consecutive angles supplementary" is for parallelograms. For an isosceles trapezoid, only the same-leg co-interior angles are supplementary (because the bases are parallel).
  • The exterior angle sum of any convex polygon is always 360° regardless of the number of sides.

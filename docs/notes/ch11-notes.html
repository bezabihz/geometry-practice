Chapter 11 — Areas of Polygons & Circles

Area is measured in square units. Every formula in this chapter follows from the rectangle (base × height) by splitting, doubling, or averaging. Master the four-or-five core formulas and you can derive the rest.

1 Vocabulary

Base & height
A base is a chosen side; the height is the perpendicular distance from the opposite vertex (or parallel side) to the base.
Altitude
A perpendicular segment from a vertex to the line containing the opposite side.
Apothem
In a regular polygon, the perpendicular distance from the center to a side.
Radius (of polygon)
Distance from the center to a vertex.
Sector
A "pie slice" of a circle, bounded by two radii and an arc.
Segment of a circle
Region bounded by a chord and its arc.

2 Parallelogram & Rectangle

Theorem 11-1
Area of a Rectangle
The area of a rectangle equals the product of its base and height.
A = b · h
Theorem 11-2
Area of a Parallelogram
The area of a parallelogram equals the product of a base and the corresponding perpendicular height.
A = b · h
For a parallelogram, "height" is not the slant side. It is the perpendicular distance between the two parallel sides chosen as bases.

3 Triangle

Theorem 11-3
Area of a Triangle
The area of a triangle is one-half the product of its base and corresponding height.
A = ½ · b · h

A triangle is half a parallelogram (cut along a diagonal). For any triangle with sides a, b, c and angle C between sides a and b:

A = ½ · a · b · sin C

Heron's formula (useful with all three sides known):

s = (a + b + c)/2 ; A = √[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)]

4 Trapezoid

Theorem 11-4
Area of a Trapezoid
The area equals one-half the product of the height and the sum of the two parallel bases.
A = ½ · h · (b₁ + b₂)

Equivalently, area = (midsegment) · h since the midsegment is the average of the two bases.

5 Rhombus & Kite

Theorem 11-5
Area of a Rhombus or Kite (Diagonals)
For any rhombus or kite, the area equals half the product of the diagonals.
A = ½ · d₁ · d₂
Because a rhombus is also a parallelogram, you can still use A = b·h for it. The diagonal formula is convenient when the diagonals are easy to find.

6 Regular Polygons — Apothem Formula

Theorem 11-6
Area of a Regular Polygon
The area of a regular polygon equals half the product of the apothem and the perimeter.
A = ½ · a · P

For a regular n-gon with side length s: P = n·s. The apothem can be found via trigonometry:

a = s / (2·tan(180°/n))

Useful trigonometric breakdown: a regular n-gon divides into n congruent isosceles triangles meeting at the center, each with vertex angle 360°/n.

7 Circles, Sectors, Segments

Circle

Circumference: C = 2πr = πd
Area: A = πr²

Sector (pie slice)

Sector area = (m/360°) · πr², where m = central angle (degrees)
Arc length = (m/360°) · 2πr

Segment (chord region)

Area of a segment = area of the corresponding sector − area of the triangle formed by the chord and the two radii.

A regular polygon's area formula ½ a P "becomes" the circle's πr² in the limit: as n → ∞, apothem → r, perimeter → 2πr, and A = ½·r·2πr = πr².

8 Areas of Similar Figures

Theorem 11-7
Ratio of Areas
If two figures are similar with scale factor (linear ratio) a/b, then the ratio of their areas is a²/b².
From a known area ratio, the linear scale factor is the square root: ratio of perimeters / sides = √(area ratio).

9 Composite Figures

Strategy:

  1. Decompose the figure into shapes you know (rectangles, triangles, circles, sectors).
  2. Find each area individually.
  3. Add areas of pieces that make up the shape; subtract any cut-outs.
When a region is bounded by curves (e.g. a "stadium" with two semicircular ends), look for ways to combine the half-shapes into a full circle plus a rectangle.

10 Worked Examples

Example 1 · Parallelogram
Find the area of a parallelogram with base 12 and height 7.
  1. A = 12 · 7 = 84 sq units.
A = 84
Example 2 · Trapezoid
Bases 10 and 16, height 5. Area?
  1. A = ½·5·(10 + 16) = ½·5·26 = 65.
A = 65
Example 3 · Rhombus diagonals
Diagonals 14 and 9. Area?
  1. A = ½·14·9 = 63.
A = 63
Example 4 · Regular hexagon
A regular hexagon has side 8. Find the area.
  1. Apothem: a = 8 / (2 tan 30°) = 8 / (2·(1/√3)) = 4√3.
  2. Perimeter: P = 6·8 = 48.
  3. Area: A = ½·a·P = ½·4√3·48 = 96√3 ≈ 166.3.
A = 96√3 ≈ 166.3
Example 5 · Sector
A sector with radius 6 and central angle 60°. Find the area.
  1. A = (60/360)·π·6² = (1/6)·36π = 6π ≈ 18.85.
A = 6π ≈ 18.85
Example 6 · Similar polygon areas
Two similar polygons have sides in ratio 2:5. If the smaller has area 40, find the larger.
  1. Area ratio = (2/5)² = 4/25.
  2. 40/A_large = 4/25 → A_large = 40·25/4 = 250.
A_large = 250

11 Common Pitfalls

  • Height is always perpendicular to the chosen base. Don't use a slant side as height.
  • Area units are square units (cm², in²). Don't forget to square.
  • For trapezoids, average the two bases then multiply by height. Order of operations matters.
  • The rhombus diagonal formula is ½ d₁ d₂, not d₁ d₂.
  • For regular polygons, the apothem (to a side) is shorter than the radius (to a vertex). Don't confuse them.
  • Sector area uses (m/360°) of πr²; arc length uses (m/360°) of 2πr.
  • Area ratio of similar figures is the square of the linear ratio — a frequent test trap.

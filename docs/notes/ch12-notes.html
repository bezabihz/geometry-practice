Chapter 12 — Surface Area

Surface area is the total area of every face of a 3-D solid. For solids with curved surfaces (cylinders, cones, spheres) the formulas come from "unrolling" the surface into flat pieces.

1 Vocabulary & Solid Types

Polyhedron
A 3-D solid bounded by flat polygonal faces. Edges where faces meet; vertices where edges meet.
Prism
A polyhedron with two parallel congruent bases and parallelogram lateral faces. Right prism: lateral faces are rectangles.
Cylinder
Two parallel congruent circular bases connected by a curved surface.
Pyramid
A polyhedron with one polygonal base and triangular lateral faces meeting at the apex.
Cone
A solid with a circular base and a single apex connected by a curved surface.
Sphere
The set of points in space at a fixed distance (radius) from a center.
Lateral surface area (L.A.)
The area of the sides only — does not include the bases.
Total surface area (S.A.)
Lateral area + area of the base(s).
Slant height (ℓ)
The slant distance from the base edge to the apex (for cones/pyramids). Different from the vertical height h.

2 Nets & Euler's Formula

A net is the unfolded 2-D pattern that wraps to form a solid. Computing surface area from a net is straightforward: find the area of each polygon piece and sum.

Theorem 12-1
Euler's Formula for Polyhedra
For any convex polyhedron with V vertices, E edges, and F faces: V − E + F = 2.

3 Prisms

Theorem 12-2
Lateral & Total Surface Area of a Right Prism
For a right prism with base perimeter P, base area B, and height h:
L.A. = P · h
S.A. = P · h + 2B

Reason: the lateral faces unroll into one big rectangle of width P and height h. Add the two congruent bases.

4 Cylinders

Theorem 12-3
Surface Area of a Right Cylinder
For a right cylinder with radius r and height h:
L.A. = 2πr · h
S.A. = 2πr · h + 2πr²

The lateral surface unrolls into a rectangle whose width is the circumference (2πr) and whose height is h.

5 Pyramids

Theorem 12-4
Lateral & Total Surface Area of a Regular Pyramid
For a regular pyramid with base perimeter P, slant height ℓ, and base area B:
L.A. = ½ · P · ℓ
S.A. = ½ P · ℓ + B

The lateral faces are congruent isosceles triangles each with base = side of polygon and height = slant height.

The slant height, vertical height, and apothem form a right triangle:

ℓ² = h² + a² (a = apothem)

6 Cones

Theorem 12-5
Surface Area of a Right Cone
For a right cone with radius r and slant height ℓ:
L.A. = π · r · ℓ
S.A. = π r ℓ + π r²

The lateral surface unrolls into a sector of a circle with radius ℓ. The slant, vertical height, and radius form a right triangle:

ℓ² = r² + h²

7 Spheres

Theorem 12-6
Surface Area of a Sphere
The surface area of a sphere with radius r:
S.A. = 4 π r²

For a hemisphere, total surface = ½ sphere + circular base = 2πr² + πr² = 3πr².

8 Surface Area of Similar Solids

Theorem 12-7
Ratios for Similar Solids
If two solids are similar with scale factor a/b:
  • Ratio of corresponding lengths = a/b.
  • Ratio of surface areas = a²/b².
  • Ratio of volumes = a³/b³ (covered in Ch 13).

9 Worked Examples

Example 1 · Rectangular prism
A rectangular prism has dimensions 4 × 5 × 7. Find S.A.
  1. Two of each face: 2(4·5) + 2(5·7) + 2(4·7) = 40 + 70 + 56 = 166.
S.A. = 166
Example 2 · Cylinder
A cylinder has r = 3 and h = 8. Find S.A. (in terms of π).
  1. L.A. = 2π·3·8 = 48π.
  2. 2 bases: 2π·3² = 18π.
  3. S.A. = 48π + 18π = 66π.
S.A. = 66π
Example 3 · Square pyramid
A square pyramid has base side 6 and slant height 10. Find S.A.
  1. P = 24, B = 36.
  2. L.A. = ½ · 24 · 10 = 120.
  3. S.A. = 120 + 36 = 156.
S.A. = 156
Example 4 · Cone
A cone has r = 5 and h = 12. Find the slant height, then S.A.
  1. Slant: ℓ = √(5² + 12²) = √169 = 13.
  2. L.A. = π·5·13 = 65π.
  3. Base: π·25 = 25π.
  4. S.A. = 65π + 25π = 90π.
S.A. = 90π ≈ 282.7
Example 5 · Sphere
Sphere with diameter 10. Find S.A.
  1. r = 5. S.A. = 4π·5² = 100π ≈ 314.16.
S.A. = 100π
Example 6 · Similar solids
Two similar cones have radii 4 and 6. If the smaller has S.A. = 80π, find the larger.
  1. Linear ratio = 4/6 = 2/3 ⇒ S.A. ratio = 4/9.
  2. 80π / SA_large = 4/9 → SA_large = 80π · 9/4 = 180π.
SA_large = 180π

10 Common Pitfalls

  • Slant height (ℓ) ≠ vertical height (h). Use ℓ² = h² + (a or r)² to convert.
  • Lateral area excludes the bases; total surface area includes them. Read which is requested.
  • A cylinder has two circular bases; a cone has one.
  • For pyramids, multiply lateral by ½ · P · ℓ only when the pyramid is regular. Otherwise sum individual triangle areas.
  • Sphere uses 4πr², not πr² (which is a great-circle cross-section).
  • Square units! S.A. has dimensions of length², not length³ (that's volume).
  • S.A. ratio of similar solids is the square of the linear ratio.

Chapter 10 — Circles

A circle's geometry is governed by where lines cross it: as a tangent, a chord, or a secant. The central- and inscribed-angle theorems and the segment relationships ("power of a point") let you find any missing arc, angle, or length.

1 Vocabulary

Circle
The set of all points in a plane equidistant from a given center point. The constant distance is the radius.
Chord
A segment whose endpoints lie on the circle.
Diameter
A chord passing through the center. Length = 2·radius.
Secant
A line that intersects the circle in two points.
Tangent
A line that intersects the circle at exactly one point (the point of tangency).
Arc
A portion of the circle between two points. Minor arc < 180°, major arc > 180°, semicircle = 180°.
Central angle
An angle whose vertex is the center of the circle.
Inscribed angle
An angle whose vertex is on the circle, formed by two chords.
Intercepted arc
The arc that lies in the interior of an inscribed angle (or between the sides of a central angle).
Congruent circles
Circles with the same radius.

2 Arcs & Central Angles

Postulate 10-1
Arc Addition Postulate
The measure of an arc formed by two adjacent arcs equals the sum of their measures.
Theorem 10-1
Central Angle = Intercepted Arc
In a circle, the measure of a central angle equals the measure of its intercepted arc.

Length and measure are different:

arc length = (arc measure / 360°) · 2πr
Theorem 10-2
Congruent Arcs & Chords
In the same circle (or congruent circles), two arcs are congruent if and only if their corresponding chords are congruent.

3 Chord Theorems

Theorem 10-3
Perpendicular Diameter Bisects Chord
If a diameter (or radius) is perpendicular to a chord, then it bisects the chord and its arc.
Theorem 10-4
Bisecting Chord ⇒ Perpendicular
The perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the center of the circle.
Theorem 10-5
Equidistant Chords are Congruent
In the same circle (or congruent circles), two chords are congruent if and only if they are equidistant from the center.

4 Inscribed Angle Theorems

Theorem 10-6
Inscribed Angle Theorem
An inscribed angle is half the measure of its intercepted arc.
m∠inscribed = ½ · m(intercepted arc)
Corollary 10-6a
Same Arc ⇒ Same Inscribed Angles
Inscribed angles that intercept the same arc (or congruent arcs) are congruent.
Corollary 10-6b
Inscribed Angle in a Semicircle
An angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle. (Its intercepted arc is 180°; half of 180° is 90°.)
Corollary 10-6c
Cyclic Quadrilateral
If a quadrilateral is inscribed in a circle, then its opposite angles are supplementary.

5 Tangents

Theorem 10-7
Tangent ⊥ Radius
A line is tangent to a circle if and only if it is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency.
Theorem 10-8
Two Tangent Segments from External Point
If two segments from the same external point are tangent to a circle, then they are congruent.
From an external point, you can draw exactly two tangent lines to a circle, and the segments to the points of tangency are equal in length.

6 Angles Formed by Chords, Secants & Tangents

Vertex locationAngle measure
At the center= intercepted arc
On the circle (inscribed)= ½ · intercepted arc
On the circle (tangent-chord)= ½ · intercepted arc
Inside the circle (2 chords)= ½ · (sum of intercepted arcs)
Outside the circle (2 secants, secant-tangent, or 2 tangents)= ½ · (far arc − near arc)
Theorem 10-9
Tangent-Chord Angle
If a tangent and a chord meet at the point of tangency, the angle equals half the intercepted arc.
Theorem 10-10
Two Chords Inside
If two chords intersect inside a circle, each angle equals half the sum of the two intercepted arcs.
Theorem 10-11
External Angles
The angle formed outside the circle by two secants, a secant and a tangent, or two tangents equals half the positive difference of the two intercepted arcs.

7 Segment Lengths — Power of a Point

Theorem 10-12
Two Chords (Inside)
If two chords intersect inside a circle, the product of the lengths of one chord's segments equals the product of the other's: a·b = c·d.
Theorem 10-13
Two Secants (Outside)
If two secants from an external point cross the circle, the product (outside · whole) is the same for each: a(a + b) = c(c + d).
Theorem 10-14
Secant–Tangent (Outside)
If a secant and a tangent share an external endpoint, then (tangent)² = (outer secant) · (whole secant): t² = a(a + b).

8 Equations of Circles

Theorem 10-15
Standard-Form Equation
A circle with center (h, k) and radius r has equation:
(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²

To put a general equation x² + y² + Dx + Ey + F = 0 in standard form, complete the square in x and in y.

Circle centered at origin:

x² + y² = r²

9 Worked Examples

Example 1 · Central / inscribed
Arc AB measures 80°. Find the inscribed angle that intercepts AB.
  1. Inscribed = ½·arc = ½·80° = 40°.
40°
Example 2 · Cyclic quadrilateral
ABCD is inscribed in a circle. ∠A = 95°. Find ∠C.
  1. Opposite ∠s supplementary: ∠C = 180° − 95° = 85°.
∠C = 85°
Example 3 · Two intersecting chords
Chords AB and CD cross at P inside the circle. AP = 6, PB = 8, CP = 4. Find PD.
  1. AP·PB = CP·PD ⇒ 6·8 = 4·PD.
  2. PD = 48/4 = 12.
PD = 12
Example 4 · External angle (two secants)
From point P outside the circle, two secants intercept arcs of 110° and 40°. Find the angle at P.
  1. Angle = ½·(far − near) = ½·(110 − 40) = 35°.
35°
Example 5 · Equation of a circle
Write the equation of the circle with center (−3, 2) and radius 5.
  1. Use standard form: (x − (−3))² + (y − 2)² = 5².
  2. (x + 3)² + (y − 2)² = 25.
(x + 3)² + (y − 2)² = 25
Example 6 · Complete the square
Find the center and radius of x² + y² − 6x + 8y − 11 = 0.
  1. Group: (x² − 6x) + (y² + 8y) = 11.
  2. Complete: (x − 3)² − 9 + (y + 4)² − 16 = 11.
  3. (x − 3)² + (y + 4)² = 36 ⇒ center (3, −4), r = 6.
Center (3, −4), radius 6.

10 Common Pitfalls

  • Arc measure (degrees) ≠ arc length (distance). Arc length = (m/360°)·2πr.
  • The inscribed-angle rule uses the intercepted arc, not the arc near the vertex.
  • Inside the circle: sum of intercepted arcs, then halve. Outside the circle: difference, then halve.
  • Power of a point (chords): each chord's two pieces multiplied. For secants from an external point, use outside · whole.
  • Tangent-secant: square the tangent. Don't multiply tangent by itself only once.
  • When completing the square, remember to add the same amount to both sides of the equation.
  • An inscribed angle in a semicircle is always 90° — a classic exam pattern.

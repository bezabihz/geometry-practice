What is the hypothesis of the conditional: "If it is raining, then the ground is wet"?
B is correct. In "If p, then q," the hypothesis is p (the "if" part). Here p = "it is raining."
Question 2 of 8
The contrapositive of "If p, then q" is:
C is correct. Contrapositive: "If ¬q, then ¬p." It is always logically equivalent to the original conditional. (A is the converse; B is the inverse.)
Question 3 of 8
Law of Detachment: Given "If p then q" is true AND p is true, what can you conclude?
B is correct. Law of Detachment (modus ponens): if the conditional is true and the hypothesis is true, the conclusion must be true.
Question 4 of 8
Which best describes a biconditional statement?
C is correct. A biconditional (p ↔ q) is true when both the conditional and its converse are true — written "p if and only if q."
Question 5 of 8
In a two-column proof, what goes in the right column?
B is correct. A two-column proof has Statements on the left and Reasons (postulates, definitions, theorems) on the right. Each reason justifies the corresponding statement.
Question 6 of 8
Which type of reasoning uses specific examples to form a general conclusion?
B is correct. Inductive reasoning observes specific cases and conjectures a pattern. Deductive reasoning applies general rules to reach specific conclusions — deductive proofs guarantee truth, inductive conclusions can be wrong.
Question 7 of 8
Law of Syllogism: "If p→q and q→r," then you can conclude:
A is correct. Law of Syllogism: chaining conditionals. If p→q and q→r are both true, then p→r is true. Like a logical chain.
Question 8 of 8
A conjecture is best described as:
B is correct. A conjecture is an unproven statement based on observation — it becomes a theorem only after it is formally proven. A counterexample can disprove a conjecture.

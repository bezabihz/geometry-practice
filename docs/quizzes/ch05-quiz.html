Question 1 of 8
The point equidistant from all three vertices of a triangle is the:
C is correct. The circumcenter is the intersection of perpendicular bisectors. It is equidistant from all three vertices and is the center of the circumscribed circle.
Question 2 of 8
The centroid divides each median in the ratio:
C is correct. The Centroid Theorem: the centroid is located ⅔ of the way from each vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side — that's a 2:1 ratio from vertex.
Question 3 of 8
Can a triangle have sides of lengths 3, 4, and 8?
B is correct. Triangle Inequality Theorem: the sum of any two sides must be GREATER THAN the third. 3 + 4 = 7, which is not greater than 8, so no triangle is possible.
Question 4 of 8
The midsegment of a triangle connects midpoints of two sides. It is:
A is correct. Triangle Midsegment Theorem: a midsegment is parallel to the third side and exactly half as long.
Question 5 of 8
The point equidistant from all three sides of a triangle is the:
D is correct. The incenter is the intersection of angle bisectors. It is equidistant from all three sides and is the center of the inscribed circle.
Question 6 of 8
In a triangle, the side opposite the largest angle is the:
C is correct. Angle-Side Relationship: the largest angle is opposite the longest side. The smallest angle is opposite the shortest side.
Question 7 of 8
Which center is the intersection of the altitudes of a triangle?
D is correct. The orthocenter is the intersection of the three altitudes (perpendicular segments from each vertex to the opposite side).
Question 8 of 8
A median in a triangle connects a vertex to the:
A is correct. A median goes from a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. Each triangle has exactly three medians, all meeting at the centroid.

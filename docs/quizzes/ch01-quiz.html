Question 1 of 8
How many points are needed to define a unique line?
B is correct. Any two distinct points determine exactly one line (Postulate 2-1). One point is insufficient; three or more are more than needed.
Question 2 of 8
Point M is the midpoint of segment AB. If AM = 3x + 1 and MB = 5x − 7, what is MB?
B is correct. Set AM = MB (midpoint definition): 3x+1 = 5x−7 → 8 = 2x → x = 4. MB = 5(4)−7 = 13.
Question 3 of 8
Points A, B, and C are collinear with B between A and C. If AC = 20 and BC = 7, find AB.
C is correct. Segment Addition Postulate: AB + BC = AC → AB + 7 = 20 → AB = 13.
Question 4 of 8
What is the midpoint of segment PQ if P = (2, 6) and Q = (8, −2)?
A is correct. Midpoint formula: ((2+8)/2, (6+(−2))/2) = (10/2, 4/2) = (5, 2).
Question 5 of 8
Two angles are supplementary. One angle measures 65°. What is the other?
B is correct. Supplementary angles sum to 180°. 180° − 65° = 115°. (Complementary angles sum to 90°, not 180°.)
Question 6 of 8
Which pair of angles are vertical angles when two lines intersect?
C is correct. Vertical angles are the non-adjacent (opposite) pairs formed when two lines cross. They are always congruent.
Question 7 of 8
How many planes contain a given line?
D is correct. A line lies in infinitely many planes — you can "rotate" any plane around the line to get a different plane still containing it.
Question 8 of 8
An angle bisector divides an angle into two angles each measuring (2x + 5)°. If the original angle is 70°, find x.
B is correct. Each half = 70°/2 = 35°. So 2x+5 = 35 → 2x = 30 → x = 15.

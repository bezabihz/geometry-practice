## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Volume** | Amount of space enclosed by a solid (cubic units) |
| **Prism** | Two congruent parallel bases; lateral faces are parallelograms |
| **Cylinder** | Two congruent circular bases |
| **Pyramid** | One base; triangular lateral faces meeting at apex |
| **Cone** | One circular base; comes to an apex |
| **Sphere** | All points equidistant from center |
| **Composite solid** | Combination of two or more solids |

---

<!-- _class: theorem -->

## Cavalieri's Principle

> If two solids have the **same height** and **equal cross-sectional areas** at every level, they have the **same volume**.

*This is why the volume formulas for oblique and right solids are the same.*

---

<!-- _class: theorem -->

## Theorem 13-1: Volume of a Prism

$$V = Bh$$

where $B$ = area of base, $h$ = height.

**Rectangular prism:** $V = lwh$

**Triangular prism:** $V = \frac{1}{2}bh_{\triangle} \cdot h_{\text{prism}}$

---

<!-- _class: example -->

### Example — Rectangular Prism

$l = 8$, $w = 5$, $h = 3$

$$V = 8 \times 5 \times 3 = 120 \text{ cubic units}$$

---

<!-- _class: theorem -->

## Theorem 13-2: Volume of a Cylinder

$$V = \pi r^2 h$$

**Example:** Radius $= 4$, height $= 9$:
$$V = \pi(4)^2(9) = 144\pi \approx 452.4$$

---

<!-- _class: theorem -->

## Theorem 13-3: Volume of a Pyramid

$$V = \frac{1}{3}Bh$$

where $B$ = area of base, $h$ = height.

**Note:** Volume of pyramid $= \frac{1}{3} \times$ volume of prism with same base and height.

---

<!-- _class: example -->

### Example — Square Pyramid

Base: $6 \times 6$, height $= 10$

$$B = 36, \quad V = \frac{1}{3}(36)(10) = 120$$

---

<!-- _class: theorem -->

## Theorem 13-4: Volume of a Cone

$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$$

**Note:** Volume of cone $= \frac{1}{3} \times$ volume of cylinder with same radius and height.

**Example:** Radius $= 3$, height $= 7$:
$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi(9)(7) = 21\pi \approx 66.0$$

---

<!-- _class: theorem -->

## Theorem 13-5: Volume of a Sphere

$$V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$$

**Example:** Radius $= 6$:
$$V = \frac{4}{3}\pi(216) = 288\pi \approx 904.8$$

---

## Volume Summary

| Solid | Formula |
|-------|---------|
| Prism | $V = Bh$ |
| Cylinder | $V = \pi r^2 h$ |
| Pyramid | $V = \frac{1}{3}Bh$ |
| Cone | $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$ |
| Sphere | $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$ |

**Memory tip:** Pyramid/Cone $= \frac{1}{3} \times$ corresponding Prism/Cylinder

---

## Surface Area vs. Volume

| Solid | SA | Volume |
|-------|----|----|
| Sphere | $4\pi r^2$ | $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$ |
| Cylinder | $2\pi r h + 2\pi r^2$ | $\pi r^2 h$ |
| Cone | $\pi r\ell + \pi r^2$ | $\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$ |

---

<!-- _class: example -->

### Example — Composite Solid

A cylinder (radius 4, height 6) is topped with a cone (radius 4, height 3).

$$V_{\text{cylinder}} = \pi(16)(6) = 96\pi$$
$$V_{\text{cone}} = \frac{1}{3}\pi(16)(3) = 16\pi$$
$$V_{\text{total}} = 112\pi \approx 351.9$$

## Similar Solids

> If two similar solids have scale factor $k$:

| Measurement | Ratio |
|-------------|-------|
| Linear (edge, radius, height) | $k$ |
| Area (SA, base area) | $k^2$ |
| Volume | $k^3$ |

**Example:** Scale factor $\frac{2}{3}$:
- SA ratio $= \frac{4}{9}$
- Volume ratio $= \frac{8}{27}$
